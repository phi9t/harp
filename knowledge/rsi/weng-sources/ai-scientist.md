# Automating an Ideation-to-Paper Workflow

## Problem

Captured lines 72–84 ask whether one system can coordinate the computational research lifecycle from idea formation through experiments, manuscript writing, and review.

## Core mechanism

The AI Scientist combines ideation, literature checks, code edits, experiment execution, result logging, figure critique, writing, and automated review. Its template-free experiment tree advances through investigation, hyperparameter, agenda, and ablation stages. Model weights are not the edited object.

## Reported evidence

Three generated manuscripts entered an approved workshop review. Lines 106–120 report one average score of 6.33 from scores 6, 7, and 6; the other two missed acceptance, and the authors' internal review found none at main-conference quality. Harp did not reproduce these results.

## Key limitation

The source says humans filtered outputs before submission and experiments were computational only. One of three AI manuscripts likely cleared review; separately, the venue-wide workshop acceptance rate was 70%. The authors say none met main-conference quality and list implementation, rigour, figure, and citation failures.

## Why Weng cites it

Weng lines 154 and 315 uses AI Scientist as evidence that an expert-designed harness can coordinate much of auto-research, while warning that paper production is not scientific discovery.
