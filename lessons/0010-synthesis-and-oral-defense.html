Five oral-defense prompts

  1. Causal chain: Trace one claimed gain from edited object through execution, evaluation, acceptance, persistence, and later use.
  2. Optimization ladder: Move from context artifact to context mechanism to executable harness code, naming what stays fixed.
  3. Unfamiliar-system classification: Given only a mechanism, identify edited object, update operator, evaluator, archive, and weight boundary.
  4. Adjacent-paper comparison: Compare two nearby systems without erasing differences in representation, budget, or evidence class.
  5. Evidence/claim mismatch: Find the first unsupported step between a reported result and an RSI conclusion.

What is required after a strong same-session oral defense to support durable learning?

Completion is not mastery

Same-session completion is not durable learning; later retrieval is required. Page completion records navigation, not mastery. Progress should follow retrieval evidence: delayed reconstruction, discrimination between adjacent systems, and calibrated oral defense without the reference open.
