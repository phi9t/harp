# Framing the Gap to Successor Development

## Problem

The company essay asks whether AI-assisted engineering and research could close into autonomous successor development. It explicitly says, "We are not there yet," and RSI is not inevitable.

## Core mechanism

Its proposed progression moves from coding assistance to autonomous execution, experiment choice, model training, and successor design. Humans still set goals, rubrics, and research direction. The essay frames future safety risks; it does not present a closed RSI mechanism.

## Reported evidence

Anthropic self-reports that over 80% of production-merged lines were attributed to Claude, with attribution gaps, and that lines merged per engineer per day were 8x in Q2 2026 versus 2024, explicitly not productivity. Fixed-goal small-model optimization rose from about 3x to 52x across releases, not a real-world training speedup. It also reports 97% gap recovery by agents using 800 cumulative hours and $18,000, versus 23% by two humans over about a week. Humans chose the problem and rubric, and transfer to production-scale models was not clean. Harp did not reproduce this.

## Key limitation

The essay is mutable company evidence. A biased 129-case next-step comparison is not like-for-like; on 127 moments with a strong human choice, model suggestions won about 20%. Human research taste and direction-setting remain explicit gaps.

## Why Weng cites it

Weng line 26 links Anthropic only for accelerated AI development. Harp interprets the essay as successor-development and risk framing, not experimental proof of RSI.
